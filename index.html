Doctor Trigger

A Framework for Release-Bound Face Verification

Abstract

Face swapping and generative editing enable realistic facial manipulation, while online redistribution complicates publisher oversight of circulating face images. Existing approaches establish provenance, detect manipulation, recover embedded watermarks, verify image integrity, or disrupt unauthorized face manipulation. These capabilities do not necessarily establish consistency with a specific publisher-enrolled release, limiting the evidence available for verification and human review. To address this gap, we introduce Doctor Trigger, a framework for verifying circulating faces against private publisher-held release records. Before publication, the publisher embeds a weak keyed Fourier-phase signal in each face while preserving visual fidelity and stores its spectral reference. Afterward, verification compares the circulating face with that reference and uses key projection to measure retention of the release-specific signal. Therefore, relation and key evidence jointly help publishers identify suspicious copies for review, potentially reducing the circulation of manipulated images. Experimental results across FF++, DFDC, and Celeb-DF show that spectral agreement separates tested face swaps and generative manipulations from enrolled releases, while the keyed signal distinguishes published versions from their unreleased originals under the evaluated key-preserving benign transformations. Doctor Trigger complements provenance metadata with release-specific face verification and record-linked evidence for further assessment.

Overview

Face swaps and generative edits are now realistic, and published faces get reposted, compressed and resized as they circulate. A publisher needs to answer one question: is this circulating face still the version we released?

Provenance metadata can be stripped along the way, and image-only detectors do not check a face against a specific release. Doctor Trigger lets the publisher keep a private record of each release and verify circulating copies against it.

Invisible release signal

A secret per-face key slightly shifts the Fourier phase. The released face stays visually near-identical (51.2 dB PSNR).

Two complementary checks

Face consistency catches swaps and edits. Release signal tells the published version apart from its unreleased original.

Human in the loop

Confirmed copies pass routinely. Unconfirmed ones go to a reviewer. Unavailable means inputs are missing.

02

How it works

Two stages. The publisher enrolls each face before release. Later, a requester, such as a person or platform, submits a circulating copy to the publisher's verifier and gets a status back.

1Enroll: add a weak keyed signal

A key derived from a master seed and the sample ID picks a signed pattern on a mid-frequency ring of the Fourier spectrum. The phase at those frequencies is shifted by a tiny amount, then a fidelity gate lowers the strength until the release meets the fidelity targets.

The publisher keeps a private record: the spectral reference, the key and the thresholds. The original image is not needed later.

phase shift 0.0071–0.0235 radPSNR ≥ 35 dBSSIM ≥ 0.98

2Verify: two checks against the record

Spectral agreement C compares the query's phase with the stored template: is this still the enrolled face?

Key projection Gs projects the phase change onto the enrolled key: is the release signal still there? An ideal release scores about 1, the unreleased original 0, and wrong keys cancel out on average.

3Decide and route

A query is Confirmed only when both checks pass thresholds calibrated on benign processing (at most 1% benign rejection). Otherwise it is Unconfirmed and flagged for review, or Unavailable if the record or inputs are missing.

Invisible

The release looks virtually unchanged.

51.2 dB PSNR, compared with 29.9–41.4 dB for the compared watermarking methods on the same face. Across all 7,366 enrollments the average is 51.20 dB (minimum 49.3 dB) with SSIM 1.00, and it holds at 128, 256 and 512 px on all three datasets (paper Table 6).
